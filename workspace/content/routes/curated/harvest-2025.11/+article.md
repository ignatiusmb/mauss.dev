## headlines

### brain rot

**[Denmark’s government aims to ban access to social media for children under 15](https://apnews.com/article/denmark-social-media-ban-children-7862d2a8cc590b4969c8931a01adc7f4)** \
although i support the idea, i haven't seen a good way to enforce this effectively without infringing on privacy and enforcing government ID verification for everything online and essentially destroying anonymity on the internet, which is a terrible idea. just like [the disguised return of EU chat control](https://reclaimthenet.org/the-disguised-return-of-the-eus-private-message-scanning-plot), a meaningless "right" where all the exceptions eat the rule. but i digress, let's move on.

**[A.I. and Social Media Contribute to ‘Brain Rot’](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/06/technology/personaltech/ai-social-media-brain-rot.html?unlocked_article_code=1.zU8.VdBz.nH3BgIzIQHVc)** \
the data showed that children who reported using a low amount of social media (one hour a day) to a high amount (at least three hours a day) scored significantly lower on reading, memory and vocabulary tests than children who reported using no social media.

> the only safe conclusion is that every hour a child spends scrolling through the apps takes time away from more enriching activities like reading and sleeping

the key to using A.I. in a healthier way is to try to be more mindful in how we use it, ask a chatbot to answer small questions rather than relying on it to do all the research on a broad topic, such as looking up historical dates.

**[Study finds memory decline surge in young people](https://onepercentrule.substack.com/p/under-40s-declining-memory)** \
there's a [Rising Cognitive Disability as a Public Health Concern Among US Adults](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000214226) with a total of 4,507,061 responses that were included in the analysis. the data is self-reported, but this still aligns with my experience and observations.

**[Cognitive and Mental Health Correlates of Short-Form Video Use](https://psycnet.apa.org/fulltext/2026-89350-001.html)** \
with a total of 98,299 participants represented across all studies in this review, the findings consistently indicate that increased short-form video (SFV) use is associated with negative cognitive and mental health outcomes.

> SFV use was associated with poorer cognition (attention, inhibitory control, language, memory, and working memory) and most mental health indices except body image and self-esteem.

TikTok and other SFV platforms are unlike any other social media, there's nothing else like them in terms of actively destroying your brain's ability to focus. use them enough, and your brain starts to find it irritating to focus on anything for more than a few seconds.

**[Kids who own smartphones before age 13 have worse mental health outcomes](https://abcnews.go.com/GMA/Family/kids-smartphones-age-13-worse-mental-health-outcomes/story?id=123961082)** \
study shows "early smartphone use was linked to low self-image in girls and boys", and soon: *adults who have smartphones have worse mental health outcomes*. whatever the safe level of smartphone usage is, most of us are way above it.

at this point, i'm pretty sure unregulated use of smartphones for children is worse than cigarettes or vapes. even for adults and elderly, excessive smartphone use is detrimental to mental health. i'm not sure how schools have progressed on this front, and even though i didn't enjoy it back then, i'm glad my school banned phones during school hours.

![Fireworks light up the night sky to mark New Year 2025 in Jakarta, Indonesia, on January 1, 2025](https://images.axios.com/p1nh8EY20qEp8s6TEBwy35Hpr2I=/0x371:6960x4286/1920x1080/2025/11/24/1764011113784.jpeg?w=1920 "[Jakarta is now the biggest city in the world](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/24/jakarta-tokyo-worlds-biggest-city-population)")

congratulations, i guess? the Jakarta metropolitan area is home to nearly 42 million people now, overtaking Tokyo as the world's most populous city. well, it does explain how horrible the traffic is there.

i don't think Jakarta is the first city that comes to mind when people think about a vacation or going to Indonesia, it's quite underrated in that sense. the public transportation, although not as good as Tokyo, is still the best in Indonesia. nearly everything is connected through the airport link, the metro, LRT, MRT, and TransJakarta bus system. it has the only legit high speed train in SEA that connects Jakarta and Bandung in around 40 minutes.

honestly, Jakarta's biggest problem — aside from the glaring fact that it's sinking — is the lack of marketing and obvious attractions, so not a lot of people even consider Jakarta as a travel destination.
